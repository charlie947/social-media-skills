```
Most AI GTM projects don't fail because the model was bad.

They fail in a meeting where two teams realize they've been defining "qualified" differently for a year.

Here's the trap: everyone wants to talk about the model. Nobody wants to own the plumbing. So you ship a slick agent on top of data that contradicts itself, and you get faster nonsense.

The boring stuff that actually decides it:

- Definitions: does "qualified" mean the same thing in sales, marketing, and the CRM? Usually not.
- Ownership: who fixes it when the workflow breaks at 2pm on a Tuesday? If the answer is "we'll see," it's already dead.
- Data quality: garbage in, confident garbage out. AI doesn't clean your data. It launders it.
- QA: if no human checks the first 100 outputs, you're running a science experiment on your own pipeline.
- Adoption: a workflow sales won't use is an expensive Slack notification.

The play:
1. Write the definitions down before you write a single prompt.
2. Name one owner per workflow. Not a committee.
3. Sample the first 100 outputs by hand. Every time.

The model is the 10% everyone fights over. The 90% that decides whether this works looks like janitorial work.

Boring wins. It usually does.
```
